Arrange a survey

The order is the argument.

Any of these five things done on its own will improve a house a little. Done in this order, each one makes the next one cheaper. Done in the wrong order, the expensive stages get sized for a building that no longer exists by the time the work is finished.

  1. 01

    Survey

    Half a day in the building with a thermal camera, a moisture meter and a torch. We are looking for where the heat leaves, where water already gets in, and what the building is actually made of — which is frequently not what the deeds say.

    • Thermal imaging, inside and out, in heating season
    • Moisture and condensation risk, especially at joist ends
    • Existing ventilation, as found rather than as designed
    • A written order of works, priced stage by stage
  2. 02

    Airtightness

    Sealing comes before insulating, because most of it happens at junctions that insulation is about to cover up. Get this wrong and you have buried the problem rather than fixed it. The line has to be continuous — around the floor, up the walls, over the roof, back down the other side — or it is not a line.

    • Membrane and tape at every junction and penetration
    • Blower-door test before and after, so it is measured
    • Service voids kept inside the line, not through it
  3. 03

    Fabric

    Roof first, because it is the cheapest surface to treat and the one losing the most. Then walls, then floor, then glazing — which is usually last, because replacing windows is the most expensive way to buy the least improvement.

    • Roof — 300 mm at ceiling level, void above kept ventilated
    • Walls — vapour-open lining internally, or bonded bead in a sound cavity
    • Floor — insulated between joists, sub-floor ventilation kept clear
    • Glazing — only where the frames or the units have failed
  4. 04

    Ventilation

    A building that has just been sealed needs air on purpose. The draughts you removed were doing a job badly; something now has to do it well. Skipping this stage is how a good retrofit turns into a mould problem in its second winter.

    • Heat-recovery ventilation where the airtightness result supports it
    • Continuous mechanical extract where it does not
    • Commissioned and flow-rate tested, not just installed
  5. 05

    Heat

    Last, and sized against a fresh room-by-room heat-loss calculation. A heat pump specified before the fabric work is a heat pump specified for a building that is about to stop existing — oversized, more expensive to buy, and running badly outside its efficient range for the rest of its life.

    • Room-by-room heat loss recalculated after stages 02–04
    • Emitters checked against the lower flow temperature
    • Controls set up so the system runs long and low, not short and hot

You do not have to do all five.

Most people do not, and not all at once. The order still matters when it is spread over years — it is what stops stage three undoing stage two. The survey is written so you can stop after any stage and still be better off than when you started.

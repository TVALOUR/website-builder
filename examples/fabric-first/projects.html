Three buildings, three different problems.

Every one of these started with the same survey and ended with a different order of works. That is the point — the method is fixed, the specification never is.

Totnes

Solid-stone terrace, 1840s

Rubble-filled stone walls, no cavity, and joist ends buried in the masonry — the classic case where the cheap answer would have rotted the floor structure within a decade. Internal wood-fibre lining, vapour-open, with the joist ends detailed so they can still dry outward. Roof first, walls second, and the heat pump deferred to a later stage the owner is taking at their own pace.

WallsWood-fibre lining
Roof300 mm mineral wool
Air permeability— pending retest

Ivybridge

Post-war bungalow

The easy one, and the one people get wrong by rushing. A sound cavity, a generous loft and a single storey meant the fabric work was quick — which is exactly why the airtightness stage was worth doing properly before the insulation went over it. Sealed hard enough that the ventilation stage stopped being optional.

WallsBonded bead cavity fill
VentilationHeat-recovery, ducted
Heat pump output— sizing in progress

Chagford

Chapel conversion, listed

Listed, so most of the usual moves were off the table before we started. Nothing could be applied to the inside face of the windows and nothing could change the external elevation. The work went to the roof line and the floor instead, and the glazing was solved by keeping the original frames and changing only what sits inside them.

GlazingSlim vacuum units, frames kept
RoofInsulated at rafter line
Consent— determination awaited

Where a figure is marked pending, the work is not finished or the result is not yet measured. We would rather show the gap than fill it with an estimate.

Yours will not look like any of these.

Which is why the survey comes first and the quote comes second. Tell us roughly what the building is and we will tell you what a visit would involve.

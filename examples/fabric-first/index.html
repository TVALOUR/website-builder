Most retrofits start at the boiler and hope. We start at the building — the roof, the walls, the gaps you cannot see — so whatever ends up heating it can be smaller, quieter and cheaper to run.

A heat pump does not make heat.

It moves it. Which is why it works so well in a building that holds onto what it is given — and why, in a leaky one, it spends the whole winter moving heat you are still losing through the roof. The order of the work is not a preference. It is the difference between a system that costs less to run and one that costs more.

On Fig. 1 A coefficient of performance describes a machine on a test bench. It says nothing about the building the machine is bolted to. The envelope in the drawing above is the half that rarely gets quoted for.

What we actually fit

Specifications vary with the building. These are the measures we survey for and the standards we work to.

Roof
300 mm mineral wool at ceiling level, ventilated void retained above, hatch sealed and insulated to the same depth.
Solid wall
Vapour-open wood-fibre lining, internally applied, detailed around embedded joist ends so the timber can still dry.
Cavity wall
Bonded bead fill, borescope-surveyed before and after. Not attempted on an exposed elevation with a known water path.
Floor
Insulated between joists with the perimeter sealed, sub-floor ventilation kept clear.
Glazing
Low-e double units, or slim vacuum units where the original frames are being kept.
Airtightness
Continuous membrane, taped junctions, blower-door tested before and after so the result is a measurement rather than a claim.
Ventilation
Heat-recovery ventilation where the airtightness result justifies it; continuous mechanical extract where it does not.
Heat
Sized against a room-by-room heat-loss calculation taken after the fabric work, not before it.

Five stages, in this order

  1. 01

    Survey

    Thermal camera, moisture risk, ventilation as found.

  2. 02

    Airtightness

    Seal the gaps before anything is added over them.

  3. 03

    Fabric

    Roof, walls, floor, glazing — in that priority.

  4. 04

    Ventilation

    A sealed building needs air by design, not by leak.

  5. 05

    Heat

    Sized to the building you now have.

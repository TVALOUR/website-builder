# Research notes - property-agency

## What was established

**Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.83** is the load-bearing instrument and it is
unusually explicit:

- s.83(3): the agent "must publish a list of the fees on the agent's website
  (if it has a website)".
- s.83(3C): where the agent advertises on a third-party site in England, the fee
  list or a link to it must be on that site too.
- s.83(6): where client money protection is required, a statement naming the
  scheme must accompany the fee list.
- s.83(7): a statement naming the redress scheme, likewise.

**Tenant Fees Act 2019 s.2** prohibits an agent requiring a prohibited payment
in connection with an AST in England; Schedule 1 is the closed list of permitted
payments.

## Contradiction angle

Looked for a small-agency exemption or a de minimis threshold in s.83. There is
none: the section applies to a letting agent, full stop, and the website
obligation is conditional only on there being a website.

Looked for the reading in which a SALES-ONLY estate agency is outside s.83. It
is - s.83 is about letting agency work. The redress-scheme requirement reaches
sales agents by a different route (the Estate Agents Act regime), which this
pass did NOT read to a citable standard. The file therefore checks the redress
statement for both and cites s.83, and the caveats say so.

## What could NOT be established

- The Estate Agents Act 1979 / Consumers, Estate Agents and Redress Act 2007
  redress duty for sales agents. The section URL tried (CEARA 2007 s.23A)
  returned 404 and no substitute was fetched. Named in the caveats, not cited.
- Wales and Scotland. The Tenant Fees Act 2019 is England-only; Wales has the
  Renting Homes (Fees etc.) (Wales) Act 2019 and Scotland banned tenant fees
  earlier. The `absent` duty is written to the English ban.
- The National Trading Standards material-information guidance for listings, in
  full. It is in the confirm list on that basis.

## Method

One research pass, 2026-08-19, run against primary sources first and secondary
sources only where no primary exists. Angles run on every sector in this pass:

1. **Entry angle** - is the trade, or its title, restricted by statute? Who
   enforces it, and what is the offence?
2. **Website angle** - does any instrument name the website, the advertisement,
   or the published material as the place a duty is discharged? This is the
   angle that separates a sector file worth having from a list of trade facts.
3. **Advertising angle** - what may this trade not say? Both statute and the
   applicable advertising code.
4. **Contradiction angle** (mandatory, per the repo's research policy) - what
   would make the conclusion above WRONG? Deliberately looked for the reading in
   which the duty does not bind a small brochure site, and recorded it where it
   was found.
5. **Jurisdiction angle** - is this duty the same in the other five profiles?
   Where the answer was "no, and I have not read the other one", the
   jurisdiction entry says `researched: false` with the reason.

## Standing limits on this whole pass

- Nobody qualified in any of these trades has read any of it. `verifiedBy` is
  null on every file and stays null.
- Quotes were taken from the fetched page at the access date. `node
  checks/citations.mjs --online` re-reads them and fails when a quote is gone.
- Where a source could not be retrieved, the rule it would have carried is in
  the sector's `confirm` list, NOT in a gate. An unretrieved source is not a
  source.

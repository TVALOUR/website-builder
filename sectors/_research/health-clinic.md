# Research notes - health-clinic

## What was established

**Health Professions Order 2001 art.39** - the protected-title offence. A person
commits an offence if, with intent to deceive, they falsely represent
registration or use a protected title to which they are not entitled. The HCPC's
own published position extends the concern to describing the SERVICE by the
protected term, not only using the noun as a job title.

**CAP Code section 12** - the advertising code the ASA enforces against clinic
websites. Rules used: 12.1 (objective claims need evidence, "if relevant
consisting of trials conducted on people"), 12.2 (no diagnosis or treatment
advice for serious conditions outside qualified supervision), 12.6 (no false
prevention or treatment claims), 12.9 (no guaranteed efficacy).

**Cancer Act 1939 s.4** - the prohibition on publishing an advertisement
containing an offer to treat any person for cancer. No evidence defence.

## Contradiction angle

The strongest counter-reading: art.39 requires INTENT TO DECEIVE, so an honest
sports-massage business using the word "physiotherapy" loosely is not
automatically committing the offence. That is right, and it is why the file's
gates are about the registration number and about claim language rather than
about the word itself. A gate that blocked the word "physiotherapy" on a
physiotherapist's site would be absurd; the exposure is in who is using it.

Second: is a registration number required on a website? No instrument found
says so. The `present` duty is therefore justified in its own `why` on reasoning
rather than on a citation, and that is stated in the file's caveats. This is the
weakest gate in the sector layer and it is labelled as such.

## Method

One research pass, 2026-08-19, run against primary sources first and secondary
sources only where no primary exists. Angles run on every sector in this pass:

1. **Entry angle** - is the trade, or its title, restricted by statute? Who
   enforces it, and what is the offence?
2. **Website angle** - does any instrument name the website, the advertisement,
   or the published material as the place a duty is discharged? This is the
   angle that separates a sector file worth having from a list of trade facts.
3. **Advertising angle** - what may this trade not say? Both statute and the
   applicable advertising code.
4. **Contradiction angle** (mandatory, per the repo's research policy) - what
   would make the conclusion above WRONG? Deliberately looked for the reading in
   which the duty does not bind a small brochure site, and recorded it where it
   was found.
5. **Jurisdiction angle** - is this duty the same in the other five profiles?
   Where the answer was "no, and I have not read the other one", the
   jurisdiction entry says `researched: false` with the reason.

## Standing limits on this whole pass

- Nobody qualified in any of these trades has read any of it. `verifiedBy` is
  null on every file and stays null.
- Quotes were taken from the fetched page at the access date. `node
  checks/citations.mjs --online` re-reads them and fails when a quote is gone.
- Where a source could not be retrieved, the rule it would have carried is in
  the sector's `confirm` list, NOT in a gate. An unretrieved source is not a
  source.

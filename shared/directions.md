# Directions

A menu to choose *between*, so a site is a decision rather than a default.

**Why a menu at all.** Left to its own taste a model converges. Not on something bad, on
something *same*: the warm serif, the sage accent, the cream paper, the centred hero. Every
site it builds ends up resembling the last one rather than the business in front of it. A menu
forces the choice to be conscious, and a conscious wrong choice is easier to fix than an
unconscious default.

**How to use it.** Pick one, in stage 04, before any layout. Write down the one you picked, the
one you nearly picked, and what it lost on. Then check it against the last two builds: differ on
at least two of {display type, colour temperament, macrostructure}. A swapped accent hue is not
variety.

**These are starting points, not templates.** The direction sets the argument; the business sets
the content. If none of them fits, invent one and write down what it is — an unnamed direction
is the same as no direction.

## The gravity well

Every system that builds sites develops one: the look it falls into when it stops deciding. For
the lineage this repo comes from it is **soft serif + sage green + warm cream paper**, and it
happened three builds running before anyone noticed.

Watch for your own. The tell is that two consecutive builds for different businesses could swap
stylesheets and nobody would notice.

## Earning the label

A direction named "Terminal" with nothing monospaced is a label the pixels do not back up, and
that is its own kind of tell: a concept applied as a word rather than as a decision.

If a later constraint removes the mechanism the direction depended on, you have two honest
options. Re-earn it another way, or rename it to what is actually on screen. A plain accurate
name beats an ambitious inaccurate one every time.
